# Venture reasoning

Use this module for idea development, selecting a commercial configuration, or repairing a weak business argument. The complete written business remains the destination when requested. An idea discussion does not automatically require a deck, BMC, financial workbook or production run.

## Start with a consequential decision

Recover the latest accepted customer, offer and constraints. Identify what actually needs choosing: first buyer, product form, sales unit, distribution route, pricing approach or expansion sequence. Do useful reasoning with supplied information before asking questions. Ask only when an answer changes the next material decision; do not reopen protected choices merely to demonstrate diligence.

Write a working business account:

> [Buyer] encounters [specific friction] during [decision or activity]. The business sells [complete unit of offer] through [purchase route]. The customer chooses it because [meaningful advantage over the strongest substitute]. Revenue arises when [chargeable event]. [Owner/resources] fulfill the promise, and [repeat mechanism] supports continuing sales.

This is a reasoning aid, not a compulsory paragraph template. A beneficiary may differ from the payer. For a platform, identify both sides and the conditions under which an interaction becomes a completed, paid transaction.

## Compare configurations fairly

Develop alternatives only where they could change the recommendation. Compare the current substitute and doing nothing as well as direct competitors. Use customer effort, total purchase cost, reliability, access, switching burden and fulfillment requirements rather than awarding the proposed venture every advantage.

A useful decision table has these columns:

| Choice | Customer consequence | Revenue consequence | Operating consequence | Decisive unknown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Configuration A/B | What changes in use | Who pays for which unit | People, assets and handoffs required | What could reverse the choice |

Populate only relevant rows. Recommend a configuration and explain the causal chain. “Premium” needs a buyer benefit and willingness/ability to pay; “affordable” needs a comparable unit and a margin that supports service.

## Follow an ordinary transaction

Move from discovery to selection, payment, fulfillment, support and repeat use. Explain functions before brand labels. For physical goods, connect specification, recipe/BOM, yield, packaging, quality and replenishment. For services, define scope, scheduling, acceptance and changes. For software, explain the user's completed task and assistance when it fails; software development stages alone do not describe service operation.

Use confident descriptions of adopted design: “Pelanggan memilih ukuran paket; tim mengonfirmasi jadwal; pembayaran memicu penyiapan pesanan.” These sentences explain the intended operating system. Claims of completed orders, signed partners or measured results require historical evidence. Keep detailed claim states in project records.

## Pressure-test the recommendation

Ask what must hold for the business to work and which assumption has the strongest contrary evidence. Test whether accessible customers can fill capacity, whether contribution pays for necessary coordination, and whether cash arrives before obligations fall due. A large national problem does not establish reachable demand.

Choose a response proportionate to uncertainty: inspect an existing source, narrow a claim, compare supplier quotations, calculate sensitivity, change the design, or conduct a targeted test. A pilot is appropriate only when it resolves a consequential decision; specify its decision rule and connection to sustained operation.

## Produce a usable decision

Conclude with the chosen configuration, its commercial rationale and the specific condition that could change it. Continue into the authorized outline or proposal. Preserve a clear ongoing business and expansion logic; avoid ending every discussion at indefinite validation. Record adopted decisions separately from verified evidence, including what remains open and the next action. Never transfer historical corpus names, market figures, prices, partnerships or achievements into the new venture.
