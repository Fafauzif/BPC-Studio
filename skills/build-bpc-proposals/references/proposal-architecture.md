# Proposal architecture

Official structure and counted-page definitions override this guide. Page budgets below are planning examples, not competition rules; render to confirm actual pagination. Cover, references and appendices may count and must be checked.

| Available body | Allocation starting point |
|---|---|
| 5 pages | 0.5 executive case; 1 customer and offer; 1 market/model; 1 operations/acquisition; 1 finance; 0.5 implementation/risk/conclusion |
| 10 pages | 1 summary; 1 problem/customer; 1.5 offer/use; 1.5 market/competition; 1 acquisition; 1 operations; 1.5 finance; 1 roadmap/risk; 0.5 conclusion |
| 20 pages | 1 summary; 2 customer/opportunity; 3 offer/value; 3 market/model; 2 marketing; 3 operations/team; 3 finance; 2 roadmap/risk; 1 conclusion |

Merge sections when space is tight. Never simply shrink typography. Protect the transaction, customer choice, revenue identity, fulfillment and decisive economics. Move long derivations rather than essential interpretation.

## Executive summary

State the complete business case in connected paragraphs. Start with the specific opportunity and offer, then explain why the customer chooses it, how value becomes revenue and what supports delivery. Select one or two consequential financial results and interpret them. Close with a credible operating and expansion path. Write this after the model is coherent, then place it first. Do not open with a generic definition of entrepreneurship or a list of section names.

## Customer and opportunity

Narrow the relevant condition into the customer's actual decision. Explain the inconvenience, spending, risk or missed value and how customers address it now. Use a scoped source or supplied research; do not invent a respondent or turn national prevalence into local willingness to pay. The transition should show why the current tradeoff creates room for the offer.

## Offer and customer experience

Define the product/service plainly. Follow a representative transaction without pretending it happened. Explain consequential specifications through their use: what the modular part enables, what the service handoff removes, what the quality control protects. Compare credible substitutes on customer-relevant criteria. Put a long technical specification in an appendix when the main text retains the reason customers care.

## Market and business model

Specify user, payer, geography, use occasion, reachable first market and why it is first. Interpret market exhibits instead of stacking TAM/SAM/SOM figures. Connect channel reach and capacity to sales volume. State the main revenue engine and timing of earning/payment. Add supporting streams only when they have a distinct buyer, deliverable, cost and realistic role.

## Acquisition and repeat purchase

Explain the route from discovery to evaluation, first purchase, fulfillment, repeat use and referral. Choose a focused channel mix with reason and owner. Branding supports memory and trust; marketing creates qualified demand. A logo or content calendar alone explains neither conversion nor retention.

## Operations and team

Follow order/material/information flow through accountable roles. Connect promised lead time to staffing, production capacity, partners, QC and contingencies. Distinguish a required partner type from a signed partner. Team roles must cover the workload in the financial plan. Expansion needs a repeatable operating unit, not only an ambition list.

## Financial case

Present units, prices, variable cost, contribution and fixed capacity before annual totals. Then show profit and cash separately, funding need and a decisive downside. Explain the headroom and constraint. Use native tables for exact schedules and a chart for a relationship. Avoid NPV as a substitute for explaining payment and delivery.

## Implementation, risk and conclusion

Milestones contain output, owner, resource, dependency and completion condition. Link early commissioning to sustained operations and expansion. Risk controls name triggers and actions. The conclusion returns to why customers want the offer and why the chosen operating configuration can earn and grow; it introduces no new unsupported market claims or achievements.

## Appendix architecture

Assign stable IDs such as A.1 Product, A.2 Customer research, A.3 Model, A.4 Operations. Choose IDs for the current project. Link body claims directly to the relevant exhibit, give a return path and keep printed labels useful. Source locators and assumptions belong beside the data they govern. An appendix must add inspectability, not repeat body prose.
