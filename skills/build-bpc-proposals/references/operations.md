# Operations and roadmap

Use this module to explain fulfillment, quality, capacity, ownership and sustained expansion. The operating plan must show how the customer receives the purchased value. An organization chart, software development cycle or list of departments cannot substitute for that explanation.

## Begin with a normal paid transaction

Identify the trigger, purchased unit and acceptance condition. Follow discovery/qualification, order or scope agreement, payment, preparation, delivery, use, support and repeat purchase. Name the owner at each handoff and explain what permits work to continue.

Use this working record for consequential steps:

| Step | Trigger and owner | Required input/resources | Output and check | Exception and timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relevant activity | What starts it; who has authority | People, data, materials, money | Evidence that the promise is met | Who resolves delay, rejection or failure |

Keep the main narrative readable; place detailed procedures in an appendix when helpful. A compact owned workflow should support prose, not replace the business explanation.

For a product, connect specifications, BOM, suppliers, receiving, production, yield, packaging, storage, shipment and replenishment. For a service, define boundaries, scheduling, scope changes and customer acceptance. For a platform, follow each side through matching, agreement, payment, fulfillment and dispute resolution. For software, describe the completed user task and support route as well as maintenance.

## Make quality an operating choice

Specify what is inspected, how often, by whom, against which acceptance criterion, and what happens to rejected work. Separate incoming-material checks, process checks and final release. Include traceability, storage or safety requirements when material to the offer.

Use measurable definitions rather than unowned targets: defect units divided by inspected units, fulfillment time measured from a defined trigger, or complaints resolved within a defined service window. Do not invent test results. “Tim memeriksa berat sebelum menyegel kemasan” describes an adopted procedure; “seluruh produk telah lolos uji” requires records.

## Reconcile capacity and economics

Calculate output from available machines, operators, productive hours, cycle time, yield and bottlenecks. Check recipe totals and unit conversions. Compare that output with the sales schedule by period; selling fifty units cannot rely on a twenty-unit production limit without inventory, outsourcing or added resources.

Map workload to staffing and authority. Include supplier lead times, minimum orders, downtime and founder availability when consequential. Tie production and service volumes to direct costs, fixed resources, inventory, receivables and working capital. A payment-after-harvest or payment-after-completion promise changes cash needs even when accounting profit looks attractive.

Use the finance module for the canonical model. Narrative, tables and roadmap must draw from the same capacity and timing assumptions.

## Design exceptions and expansion

Select failures that can break the customer promise: missing inputs, rejected batches, late delivery, payment delay, cancellation, technical failure or disputed completion. Specify detection, decision owner, customer communication, remedy and financial consequence. Avoid a long generic risk matrix disconnected from daily work.

Build the roadmap around capability releases and operating outcomes. For each stage name owner, resources, timing/dependency, output, and an exit condition that permits the next commitment. A targeted trial resolves a named uncertainty; ongoing service and expansion continue beyond it.

Explain what changes when demand doubles: staffing, equipment, supplier capacity, manager span, cash, support and quality. Expansion repeats a paid and staffed operating unit. Geography or fundraising labels alone do not constitute growth logic.

## Write and verify

Describe the selected system confidently in Indonesian by default, without manufacturing a history of installations, customers or agreements. Check that every major promise has resources, an owner, a quality condition and an exception route. Repair contradictions before polishing diagrams; document exact unresolved dependencies in the project record.
